Emergency Fund — How Much You Need and How to Build It

WHAT IS AN EMERGENCY FUND?
An emergency fund is a dedicated pool of liquid cash kept aside exclusively for unexpected, urgent expenses. It is NOT an investment — it should sit in a savings account or liquid mutual fund where you can access it within 24 hours.

WHY AN EMERGENCY FUND IS NON-NEGOTIABLE
Without an emergency fund, any unexpected expense — a medical bill, job loss, car repair, family emergency — forces you to:
1. Take an expensive personal loan (12–24% interest)
2. Swipe a credit card and pay 36–42% annual interest
3. Break long-term investments at a loss
4. Borrow from friends or family (damages relationships)

An emergency fund is financial insurance. You pay the premium by keeping this money in a low-interest account instead of investing it. The peace of mind is worth it.

HOW MUCH DO YOU NEED?
The standard recommendation is 3 to 6 months of your total monthly expenses (not income).

Use 3 months if:
- You are in a stable, salaried job in a large company
- You have a second earner in your household
- You have very low fixed monthly obligations

Use 6 months if:
- You are a freelancer or have irregular income
- You are the sole earner in your household
- Your job sector is volatile (startups, media, real estate)
- You have high fixed EMIs or loans

Use 9–12 months if:
- You run your own business
- You are planning a career switch
- You have dependents with serious health conditions

HOW TO CALCULATE YOUR EMERGENCY FUND TARGET
Step 1: Add up all essential monthly expenses:
  - Rent/EMI: ₹XX,XXX
  - Groceries: ₹XX,XXX
  - Utilities (electricity, internet, gas): ₹X,XXX
  - Insurance premiums: ₹X,XXX
  - Minimum loan repayments: ₹X,XXX
  - Transport: ₹X,XXX
Step 2: Multiply by 3 (or 6 for freelancers).
This is your emergency fund target.

Example: Monthly essential expenses = ₹35,000
- Minimum emergency fund (3 months): ₹1,05,000
- Recommended emergency fund (6 months): ₹2,10,000

WHERE TO KEEP YOUR EMERGENCY FUND
Best options in India:
1. High-interest savings account (e.g., IDFC FIRST Bank, Kotak 811 — up to 7% p.a.)
2. Liquid mutual funds (slightly higher return, 1-day redemption)
3. Overnight funds (very low risk, high liquidity)

Do NOT keep in: Fixed deposits (premature withdrawal penalty), equities (can fall 30%), or physical cash at home (no interest, theft risk).

HOW TO BUILD YOUR EMERGENCY FUND
If you have no emergency fund, start immediately — even ₹500/month counts.
Step 1: Open a separate savings account labelled "Emergency Fund Only."
Step 2: Set up an auto-transfer on salary day — treat it like a bill you must pay.
Step 3: Use windfalls (bonuses, tax refunds, gifts) to accelerate contributions.
Step 4: Once you hit your target, stop contributing and redirect to investments.
Step 5: After you use the fund for a real emergency, rebuild it immediately before resuming investments.

WHAT COUNTS AS AN EMERGENCY?
YES: Medical bills, sudden job loss, urgent car/appliance repair, emergency travel.
NO: Sale discounts, vacation, wedding gifts, planned purchases. These should be budgeted separately.
