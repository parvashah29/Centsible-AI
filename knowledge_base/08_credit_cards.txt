Credit Cards — Smart Usage, Traps to Avoid, and Building Credit Score

CREDIT CARDS: TOOL VS TRAP
A credit card is a 0% short-term loan IF you pay the full balance every month. It becomes one of the most expensive debt instruments in the world (36–42% per annum) the moment you carry a balance. The difference between these two outcomes is entirely determined by paying in full vs. paying minimums.

THE GOLDEN RULE: Always pay the full outstanding balance by the due date. No exceptions.

THE CREDIT CARD TRAP — HOW IT WORKS
Credit card companies make money from:
1. Revolving interest: When you pay only the minimum (usually 5% of balance), the remaining 95% accrues interest at 3–3.5% per MONTH (36–42% per year).
2. Annual fees: Typically ₹500–₹5,000/year for premium cards.
3. Late payment fees: ₹500–₹1,300 for any missed payment.
4. Foreign transaction fees: 1.5–3.5% on overseas transactions.
5. Cash advance fees: 2.5% + 42% annual interest from day 1 (no grace period). Never use a credit card for cash withdrawals.

Minimum payment trap example:
Balance: ₹50,000 on a credit card at 3.5%/month interest.
If you pay only ₹2,500/month (minimum 5%), it will take 4+ years to clear and you will pay ₹50,000+ in interest — more than the original balance.

HOW TO USE CREDIT CARDS SMARTLY
1. Use for regular essential spending (groceries, fuel, online purchases) to earn rewards.
2. Set up AUTO-PAY for the full outstanding balance — not just the minimum.
3. Keep credit utilisation below 30% of your credit limit (good for credit score).
4. Never use more than 1–2 cards to keep tracking simple.
5. Check your statement monthly for fraudulent charges.

BUILDING A GOOD CREDIT SCORE (CIBIL)
Your CIBIL score (300–900) affects your ability to get loans and the interest rate you are offered. A score above 750 is considered excellent.

Factors that affect CIBIL score:
1. Payment history (35% weight): Pay all EMIs and credit card bills on time. One missed payment drops your score by 50–100 points.
2. Credit utilisation (30%): Keep card usage below 30% of limit. High utilisation signals financial stress.
3. Credit history length (15%): Older accounts boost your score. Do not close your oldest credit card.
4. Credit mix (10%): Having a mix of secured (home loan) and unsecured (personal loan, credit card) debt is positive.
5. New credit inquiries (10%): Each loan application creates a "hard inquiry" which temporarily drops your score. Do not apply for multiple cards simultaneously.

How to build credit from zero:
1. Get a secured credit card (against a fixed deposit — most banks offer these).
2. Use it for small monthly expenses (₹1,000–₹3,000).
3. Pay the full balance every month for 12 months.
4. After 12 months, you will have a credit score above 700 and can apply for a regular card.
