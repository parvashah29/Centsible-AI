Debt Repayment Strategies — Avalanche vs Snowball Method

UNDERSTANDING YOUR DEBT
Before choosing a strategy, list all your debts:
- Type (credit card, personal loan, car loan, education loan, home loan)
- Outstanding balance
- Interest rate (annual percentage rate)
- Minimum monthly payment

Indian debt interest rates for reference:
- Credit card: 36–42% per annum (HIGHEST — always priority to eliminate)
- Personal loan: 10–24% per annum
- Car loan: 8–12% per annum
- Education loan: 7–11% per annum (often tax-deductible under Section 80E)
- Home loan: 8–10% per annum (also tax-deductible — do NOT overpay this aggressively)

THE DEBT AVALANCHE METHOD (Mathematically Optimal)
Rule: Always pay extra towards the debt with the HIGHEST interest rate first.
Minimum payments go to all other debts. Every extra rupee goes to the highest-rate debt.

Steps:
1. List debts from highest to lowest interest rate.
2. Pay the minimum on all debts.
3. Put every extra rupee towards Debt #1 (highest rate).
4. When Debt #1 is cleared, add its minimum payment to extra payments for Debt #2.
5. Repeat (this "avalanche" effect grows your firepower).

Advantages: Saves the most money in total interest paid. Mathematically superior.
Disadvantages: Can feel slow if your highest-rate debt has a large balance — motivation can drop.

THE DEBT SNOWBALL METHOD (Psychologically Effective)
Rule: Pay off the debt with the SMALLEST BALANCE first, regardless of interest rate.

Steps:
1. List debts from smallest to largest balance.
2. Pay the minimum on all debts.
3. Put every extra rupee towards the smallest balance (Debt #1).
4. When cleared, roll its payment to Debt #2.
5. Each payoff creates a "win" that motivates you to continue.

Advantages: Faster wins, higher motivation, better for people who struggle with discipline.
Disadvantages: You may pay more in total interest compared to the Avalanche method.

WHICH SHOULD YOU CHOOSE?
- If you are disciplined and motivated by numbers: use Avalanche.
- If you need quick emotional wins to stay on track: use Snowball.
- Both methods work — the best method is the one you will actually stick to.

SPECIAL INDIAN CONTEXT
1. Credit card debt in India carries one of the highest interest rates in the world (36–42%). If you carry a balance, this is your single most expensive financial mistake. Clear it immediately — even if it means selling investments.
2. Never invest in the stock market while carrying credit card debt. A 12% equity return cannot beat a 40% debt cost.
3. Education loan interest (Section 80E): You can claim the interest paid on education loans as a deduction for up to 8 assessment years. Do not overpay this loan until you have maximised this tax benefit.
4. Home loan (Section 80C + 24b): Principal repayment (up to ₹1.5 lakh) qualifies under 80C. Interest (up to ₹2 lakh) qualifies under Section 24b. This massively reduces the effective cost of a home loan.

DEBT REPAYMENT EXAMPLE
Debts:
- Credit card: ₹40,000 at 40% p.a.
- Personal loan: ₹1,20,000 at 15% p.a.

Avalanche order: Credit card first (higher rate).
Monthly extra payment: ₹5,000

Month 1: ₹5,000 → credit card. Balance: ₹35,000.
...continue until credit card is cleared (≈ 8–9 months).
Then redirect entire ₹5,000 + old minimum to personal loan.

By using avalanche, you save significantly more interest vs. targeting the personal loan first.
